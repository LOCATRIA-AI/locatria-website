# AI Content Brief Workflow for Local Businesses

"Write me an article about X" is not a content strategy — it's a prompt with no foundation underneath it. This guide introduces the Content Brief: the structured, reusable document that sits between research and writing, defining exactly what a piece of content should accomplish, for whom, based on what evidence, and within what boundaries — before a single word of the actual article gets drafted.

## Key Takeaways

- A Content Brief is not an outline, a keyword list, or a prompt — it's a structured specification defining why content exists, who it's for, what evidence supports it, what it must and must not claim, and what a writer or AI system should actually produce.
- The Content Brief sits between research and content production: research answers "what do we know?", the brief answers "what are we going to create from that knowledge?"
- A good brief explicitly distinguishes verified information from a content idea, an assumption, and an item still requiring verification — never letting these blur together.
- Content boundaries (what must NOT be included — unsupported claims, guarantees, invented statistics, individualized professional advice) are just as important as content requirements, and are frequently the section skipped by businesses that later regret it.
- The brief should stay simple enough for a founder-operated business to actually use — this is a lightweight governance tool, not a 50-page bureaucratic document.
- A completed, approved Content Brief becomes a genuine control layer for content integrity — a reference point to check the final article against, not just a planning exercise that's forgotten once drafting begins.

## 1. Introduction: Why Content Briefs Matter

A local business might have real research, genuine customer questions, a keyword list, and good source material — and still produce weak content, because nobody actually defined what the final piece was supposed to accomplish before writing began. Research gathers evidence. A Content Brief turns that evidence into a clear specification. Skipping this step is exactly how a business ends up with a technically accurate article that still somehow misses the point, wanders off-topic, or fails to answer the question a real reader actually came with.

A good Content Brief creates alignment before writing begins — between the business goal, the audience's actual needs, the verified evidence available, and what the final content is supposed to deliver.

---

## 2. What Is a Content Brief?

A **Content Brief** is a structured document that defines the purpose, audience, scope, evidence, content requirements, structure, risks, and success criteria for a content asset — completed before production begins.

**What it is NOT:** it is not the article itself, not just a title, not just an outline, not just a keyword list, not an AI prompt, and not a content calendar. Each of these is a different, narrower tool — a Content Brief sits above and informs all of them.

---

## 3. Why Local Businesses Need Content Briefs

Small teams face practical risks: inconsistent content quality, repeated research, generic AI text, unclear review ownership, excessive revisions, and unverified claims slipping through. A Content Brief helps reduce every one of these problems by making explicit decisions before writing starts.

## 5. Content Brief vs. Outline vs. Prompt

| | Content Brief | Outline | AI Prompt |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What it defines** | Full specification — why, for whom, evidence, boundaries | Structure — section sequence only | Instructions for AI to generate text |
| **Scope** | Broad — goal, audience, research, risks, SEO | Narrow — structural sequence | Narrow — specific generation task |
| **Timing** | Created after research, before writing | Derived from brief | Used during drafting |
| **Standalone?** | Yes — actionable for any writer | No — lacks why and evidence | No — risks generic output without brief |

---

## Step 1 — Define the Business Goal

Ask directly: what business problem should this content solve? Define whether the piece explains a service, answers recurring questions, supports local visibility, or builds topical authority.

---

## Step 2 — Define the Audience

Answer clearly: Who is this for? What do they need? What do they already know? What decision are they trying to make?

---

## Step 3 — Define the Primary Question

A strong brief centers on one clear, specific question (e.g., "How can a local business make its business information easier for AI systems to understand?").

---

## Step 4 — Define Secondary Questions

Expand the primary question into supporting sub-questions (What? Why? How? When? Who? Where? What to avoid?).

---

## Step 5 — Define the Core Answer

Define a preliminary, evidence-grounded conclusion answer upfront to guide the writer/AI.

---

## Step 6 — Convert Research Into Content Requirements

```
RESEARCH FINDING → CONTENT REQUIREMENT
```

Map every verified research finding to a concrete section, explanation, or asset in the brief.

---

## Step 7 — Define What Must Be Included

List required definitions, workflows, tables, checklists, FAQs, and warnings to prevent essential content from disappearing during drafting.

---

## Step 8 — Define What Must NOT Be Included

Define firm boundaries: no unsupported claims, no guarantees, no invented statistics, no unverified facts, no individualized medical/legal/financial advice.

---

## Step 9 — Define Sources and Evidence

Carry forward primary sources, secondary sources, URLs, dates, and verification statuses so writers do not rediscover research from scratch.

---

## Step 10 — Define SEO and GEO Requirements

Specify primary topics, related concepts, entity references, user intent, and structured information without resorting to mechanical keyword stuffing.

---

## Step 11 — Define Content Structure

Define introduction purpose, main section sequence, placement of examples/tables, FAQ location, and final takeaways.

---

## Step 12 — Define Internal Knowledge Relationships

Identify existing published content to link to meaningfully:

```
Current Article → Related Guide → Related Workflow → Related Checklist
```

---

## Step 13 — Define CTA and Conversion

Match the call to action to user intent (e.g., pointing educational guides toward related workflows or diagnostic checklists).

---

## Step 14 — Define Maintenance Requirements

Identify dynamic facts, update triggers, and recurring review schedules.

```
PUBLISH → MONITOR → REVIEW → UPDATE
```

## 35. Content Brief Quality Checklist

- [ ] Clear business goal and specific audience defined
- [ ] Primary question and secondary sub-questions clear
- [ ] Core answer preliminary statement recorded
- [ ] Required content sections and formats defined
- [ ] Prohibited content boundaries explicitly listed
- [ ] Sources, URLs, dates, and claim verification recorded
- [ ] High-risk claims flagged and reviewer assigned
- [ ] Approval status explicitly marked APPROVED before production

---

## 36. Common Content Brief Mistakes

1. Creating brief before research
2. Making brief only a keyword list or outline
3. Making brief too vague or overly complicated
4. Not defining audience, primary question, or core answer
5. Not recording sources or identifying risky claims
6. Letting AI invent missing info
7. Omitting content boundaries
8. Forgetting internal links or maintenance update triggers
9. Creating CTAs disconnected from intent
10. Producing content without brief approval

## 38. Measuring Content Brief Quality

Track brief approval rate, revision rate after drafting, content rework rate, missing section rate, source coverage, content drift rate, and percentage of articles with approved briefs.

---

## 39. What AI Can and Cannot Do

**AI CAN:** Structure research into brief formats, generate draft briefs, organize questions, map research to requirements, and audit brief completeness.  
**AI CANNOT GUARANTEE:** Factual, legal, medical, financial, or Fair Housing accuracy, search rankings, citations, or leads. Human approval remains mandatory.

## FAQ

### What is a content brief?

A Content Brief is a structured document defining the purpose, audience, scope, evidence, content requirements, structure, risks, and success criteria for a piece of content before production begins.

### What is the difference between a content brief and an outline?

An outline defines structural sequence. A Content Brief defines why content exists, for whom, based on what evidence, and within what boundaries.

### Can AI create a content brief?

AI can help organize research into a brief format, but human review and approval are mandatory before drafting begins.

### How does a content brief support content integrity?

It provides an explicit reference benchmark against which finished Markdown, JSON, and HTML can be validated for completeness and accuracy.
